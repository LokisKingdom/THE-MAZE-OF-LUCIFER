DEVLOG — August 6–7, 2026: The Economy Comes Alive

Today was one of those development days where a collection of separate experiments suddenly started looking like an actual interconnected game. We began with the desktop jukebox and turned listening to music into the foundation of a persistent economy: playing a song for at least fifteen seconds earns a small gold reward, while staying through the entire track earns a much larger payout, with safeguards against simply seeking through songs to farm money. From there we fought through the considerably less glamorous problem of making that money genuinely persistent and useful across the different parts of Loki's Kingdom. The main website, Prisoner Girl Sally, and THE GYPSIE FORTUNETELLER now recognize the same player wallet within their respective platform, meaning gold earned by listening to music can actually be carried into the characters' worlds and spent. The Fortuneteller herself became a functioning economic sink: every reading costs gold and her price increases as the player continues consulting her, eventually making repeated readings extraordinarily expensive and, in the extreme long term, effectively unaffordable. Sally received the much larger expansion. We built an actual feeding and purchasing system into her persistent state, complete with a server-controlled food catalogue ranging from basic bread and soup through BBQ pork and rice and expensive steak, tempura and sushi, along with alcoholic drinks of varying quality. Food reduces hunger and can make Sally happier and regard the player more kindly, while better and more extravagant provisions produce greater benefits. The system also understands context: feeding someone who is already full does not endlessly generate kindness, and deliberately continuing to force food and drink on Sally at maximum fullness crosses over into cruelty, fear and eventually physical harm. That gives what could have been a trivial "Feed Sally" button consequences in both directions, while establishing the beginnings of the larger resident-care system we discussed—food, health, possessions, clothing, furniture, room improvements and eventually increasingly ridiculous luxuries for a resident whose tastes can become an enormous drain on the player's musical fortune.

The second half of the day was about bringing this new ecosystem properly onto Android. We extended the jukebox reward system into the native music player while deliberately keeping Android and desktop as separate player identities: the Android jukebox, Sally and the Fortuneteller share one persistent Android economy, but the phone is not an account synchronized with the desktop browser. We added anti-cheat playback tracking, fifteen-second and full-song rewards, persistent Android visitor identity, and fixed an older likes-system annoyance along the way so the heart now refreshes correctly when Media3 naturally advances to the next song instead of only when a track is manually selected. The existing Android WebView became the bridge connecting the native jukebox economy to both AI characters. Sally's native text-to-speech bridge was extended to the Fortuneteller as well, fixing her silence inside the Android app while retaining ordinary browser speech on desktop. Finally, we made the economy visible: the Android player now displays the player's current GOLD total directly over the interface and updates it from the authoritative server balance as rewards arrive. That initially exposed one last Android-specific layout problem—the gold counter was hiding beneath the 5G/Wi-Fi/battery status icons while the music controls were being crushed underneath the navigation area—so we added proper system-bar inset handling, allowing the whole interface to respect the usable screen area on the phone. After a long chain of Worker deployments, database bindings, shared-state debugging, Kotlin changes, WebView integration, XML repairs and physical-device testing, it is now working. Listen to music → earn gold → carry that gold into the characters' worlds → spend it on readings or caring for Sally. Yesterday these were largely separate features; tonight they form the first real gameplay loop connecting the jukebox, the persistent AI characters, the website and the Android app.
