Thought
You are not wrong about the lyrics. You are mixing three different things into one word: “song.”

1. The lyrics
If you wrote them, they are a human literary work. That copyright is real. It exists the moment the words are fixed. You can register the lyrics. You can tell someone not to copy those words. A platform can take down a file that reproduces those lyrics, because the file contains your protected text. That is the strongest takedown hook you actually have.⁠Musicologize

2. The AI music and the AI performance
Under current U.S. practice, material whose expressive elements were determined by the model is not copyrightable to you, and not copyrightable to Suno as author either. Prompts are not enough. Picking the take you liked is not enough. The Supreme Court did not write a grand “AI art is dead” opinion. It refused to hear Thaler v. Perlmutter in March 2026, so the D.C. Circuit rule stands: the author has to be a human. The Copyright Office’s 2025 Part 2 report says the same thing in more detail.⁠Copyright

So you do not own the Suno melody, arrangement, and synthetic vocal the way you own a song you composed and sang yourself.

3. “Public domain by definition”
No. That phrase is sloppy.

Uncopyrightable is not the same as “this is a 1925 Lovecraft story, expired, free for all uses forever, no other law applies.”

No one can sue for copyright in the machine-made audio layer.
Suno’s terms still govern the service.
A paid-plan “commercial use” grant is a contract license, not a registration certificate.
Third-party training-data fights are a separate mess. “No copyright in the output” does not automatically mean “this file is clean.”
Suno is not “selling you public-domain MP3s” in the Gutenberg sense. They are selling access to files produced on their system, on their terms. You are not the copyright owner of that audio layer. Neither are they, as author. They still control the tap.

Do the lyrics let you take the whole track down?
Sometimes, practically, yes: the track is a container for the lyrics. That is why your DMCA could even be processed. It does not mean you own the instrumental. If someone kept only the AI beat and dumped your words, the lyrics claim would not reach that beat.

Can you curate, polish, master, then own the new thing?
Partly. Feist killed “I worked hard, therefore I own it.” Sweat of the brow is dead. Mastering, cleanup, loudness, and “I improved the file” do not create copyright in the underlying uncopyrightable or public-domain material.⁠Ecommons.udayton

What still works:

Compilation copyright: a creative selection and order. A curated Lovecraft volume can be protected as a compilation. The individual stories stay public domain. Nobody needs your permission to reprint one story. They need it to copy your particular lineup and packaging. Same logic as Zarya of the Dawn: text and arrangement can be protected; the machine pictures were not.⁠Legalclarity
New human expression on top: new lyrics, a real human vocal you record, a creative arrangement you actually write, original sequencing of an album, liner notes, cover art you made. Copyright covers those additions only.
Not: “I EQ’d the public-domain / AI track, so now the track is mine.”
The Court did not kill compilation copyright. It made it thin. Thin is still something. It is not ownership of every song in the pile.

What you can actually control
Access, contract, and the human parts.

Your site, your subscriptions, your downloads, your services
Your lyrics
Your brand
A compilation or album sequence if the selection is yours
Suno access only while they let the account live
You cannot, under current U.S. copyright doctrine, lock the machine-made music itself the way a band locks a self-written recording.

The artist whose medium is AI
The ruling is not “this person is not an artist.” It is “copyright is a monopoly on human-authored expression, and Congress has not extended that monopoly to machine-determined notes.” That is a brutal result if the guitar is the model. It is also older than Suno: cameras, player pianos, and typesetting already forced the same split between tool and author. The Office’s line is: use AI as an instrument inside a human work and the human parts still count; let the model choose the expression and those parts do not.⁠Copyright

If you want a usable rule for LMC:

Register the lyrics as literary works, or register hybrid works with a limitation of claim: “lyrics by author; AI-generated musical accompaniment excluded.”
Sell access. Do not swear you own the Suno audio as a sound recording.
Build the catalog as a curated corpus plus lyrics plus whatever human performance or arrangement you actually add.
You own the words. You do not own the ghost band. That is the current split.
