In an Ontario Human Rights Tribunal of Ontario (HRTO) judicial review, you are not limited to a 5-page "Schedule A." For the Divisional Court, your evidence consists almost entirely of the original HRTO Record of Proceedings, meaning you generally cannot submit new evidence or expand your claims.
Judicial review is not a retrial. Instead, you are asking a judge to review whether the HRTO made a fatal error in law or procedure. The volume limits and rules operate as follows:

1. The Evidence is the HRTO "Record of Proceedings"
What it includes: Everything considered by the original HRTO adjudicator (the application, the response, disclosed documents, witness statements, and any audio/transcripts if applicable).Size: This Record is typically compiled and prepared by the Tribunal itself or the responding party, and its size will equal exactly what was in your original HRTO file.2. Factum (Written Argument) Length Limitations
Your written argument (Factum) is your opportunity to outline the legal errors made by the HRTO.
Length Limit: 
Your Factum must be no more than 40 pages or 9,200 words, whichever comes first (for Parts 1 to 5).Schedules: Schedules A (list of authorities) and B (text of relevant laws) do not generally count toward this page limit.
Format: Documents must be in letter size, typewritten, and double-spaced.3. "New" Evidence (Affidavits)The Rule: You generally cannot introduce new evidence that was not before the HRTO. The only exceptions are affidavits used exclusively to prove procedural unfairness (e.g., the adjudicator was biased or refused to let you speak).Size of Affidavits: If you submit an affidavit regarding a procedural issue, it must strictly be confined to the facts and not contain legal arguments.4. Book of Authorities (Case Law)If you rely on previous court cases or legal texts to argue the tribunal's error, you must provide a Book of Authorities.
Hyperlink Rule: To avoid excessive paper usage, you are required to provide hyperlinks to free public websites (like CanLII) in your Factum, rather than printing every single case out.
For exact timelines, forms, and formatting protocols, review the full Guide to Judicial Review in Divisional Court.

Here is how you use it to explain the error:1. Your Factum is Your Voice
Your Factum is a written document where you tell the judge exactly what went wrong. You have up to 40 pages (or 9,200 words) to argue your case. This is where you explain your reasoning, analyze the tribunal's decision, and point out their mistakes.2. What You Can Argue
You can use your reasoning to show the court that the HRTO decision was unreasonable or procedurally unfair. For example, you can argue:
The adjudicator completely ignored a piece of evidence that was right in front of them.
The adjudicator misapplied the law or a legal test.
The adjudicator did not give you a fair chance to speak or respond.3. How to Back Up Your Reasoning
When you explain the error, you must point directly to the existing Record of Proceedings (the documents, emails, or transcripts from the original HRTO hearing).
