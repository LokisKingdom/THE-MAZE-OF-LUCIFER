DEVLOG — August 6–7, 2026: The Economy Comes Alive

Today was one of those development days where a collection of separate experiments suddenly started looking like an actual interconnected game. We began with the desktop jukebox and turned listening to music into the foundation of a persistent economy: playing a song for at least fifteen seconds earns a small gold reward, while staying through the entire track earns a much larger payout, with safeguards against simply seeking through songs to farm money. From there we fought through the considerably less glamorous problem of making that money genuinely persistent and useful across the different parts of Loki's Kingdom. The main website, Prisoner Girl Sally, and THE GYPSIE FORTUNETELLER now recognize the same player wallet within their respective platform, meaning gold earned by listening to music can actually be carried into the characters' worlds and spent. The Fortuneteller herself became a functioning economic sink: every reading costs gold and her price increases as the player continues consulting her, eventually making repeated readings extraordinarily expensive and, in the extreme long term, effectively unaffordable. Sally received the much larger expansion. We built an actual feeding and purchasing system into her persistent state, complete with a server-controlled food catalogue ranging from basic bread and soup through BBQ pork and rice and expensive steak, tempura and sushi, along with alcoholic drinks of varying quality. Food reduces hunger and can make Sally happier and regard the player more kindly, while better and more extravagant provisions produce greater benefits. The system also understands context: feeding someone who is already full does not endlessly generate kindness, and deliberately continuing to force food and drink on Sally at maximum fullness crosses over into cruelty, fear and eventually physical harm. That gives what could have been a trivial "Feed Sally" button consequences in both directions, while establishing the beginnings of the larger resident-care system we discussed—food, health, possessions, clothing, furniture, room improvements and eventually increasingly ridiculous luxuries for a resident whose tastes can become an enormous drain on the player's musical fortune.

The second half of the day was about bringing this new ecosystem properly onto Android. We extended the jukebox reward system into the native music player while deliberately keeping Android and desktop as separate player identities: the Android jukebox, Sally and the Fortuneteller share one persistent Android economy, but the phone is not an account synchronized with the desktop browser. We added anti-cheat playback tracking, fifteen-second and full-song rewards, persistent Android visitor identity, and fixed an older likes-system annoyance along the way so the heart now refreshes correctly when Media3 naturally advances to the next song instead of only when a track is manually selected. The existing Android WebView became the bridge connecting the native jukebox economy to both AI characters. Sally's native text-to-speech bridge was extended to the Fortuneteller as well, fixing her silence inside the Android app while retaining ordinary browser speech on desktop. Finally, we made the economy visible: the Android player now displays the player's current GOLD total directly over the interface and updates it from the authoritative server balance as rewards arrive. That initially exposed one last Android-specific layout problem—the gold counter was hiding beneath the 5G/Wi-Fi/battery status icons while the music controls were being crushed underneath the navigation area—so we added proper system-bar inset handling, allowing the whole interface to respect the usable screen area on the phone. After a long chain of Worker deployments, database bindings, shared-state debugging, Kotlin changes, WebView integration, XML repairs and physical-device testing, it is now working. Listen to music → earn gold → carry that gold into the characters' worlds → spend it on readings or caring for Sally. Yesterday these were largely separate features; tonight they form the first real gameplay loop connecting the jukebox, the persistent AI characters, the website and the Android app.

# SALLY RELATIONSHIP MODEL

### Kindness

* Continued using **Kindness** as a cumulative relationship statistic.
* Kindness only increases when qualifying behavior occurs.
* Previous cruelty is not erased when kindness increases.
* Clarified that ordinary neutral conversation should not automatically count as kindness.
* Clarified that friendly humor can qualify as kindness when it genuinely functions as a friendly social interaction.

### Cruelty

* Continued using **Cruelty** as a cumulative historical statistic.
* Cruelty does not decrease simply because the player later behaves well.
* This preserves the character's history with the player.
* Clarified that disagreement alone should not count as cruelty.
* Cruelty should instead reflect behavior such as:

  * Abuse.
  * Humiliation.
  * Threats.
  * Deliberate mockery.
  * Intimidation.
  * Other clearly hostile treatment.
* Quoted or referenced cruel language should be evaluated according to context and intent rather than automatically counted as an abusive action.

### Amusement

* Continued using **Amusement** as a separate cumulative statistic.
* Amusement can increase independently from kindness.
* A joke can therefore be:

  * Funny and kind.
  * Funny and cruel.
  * Funny but otherwise neutral.
* Established a useful example rule:

  * A friendly, non-cruel joke that genuinely lands can increase kindness by approximately +1 and amusement by approximately +2.
* This makes humor mechanically meaningful without collapsing all positive social behavior into a single relationship value.

### Contradictory Relationships

* Preserved the ability for kindness and cruelty to both become high.
* This allows Sally to remember contradictory treatment.
* The player's relationship is therefore not reduced to a single positive/negative meter.
* Intended outcomes include Sally reacting differently to:

  * Consistently kind players.
  * Consistently abusive players.
  * Players who alternate between kindness and cruelty.
  * Players who are amusing but dangerous.
  * Players who suddenly become affectionate after prolonged mistreatment.
* Sudden kindness following substantial cruelty should not instantly restore trust.
* The system can instead interpret this as emotional inconsistency or behavioral whiplash.

There isn't supposed to be one giant bar labelled:

**SALLY LIKES YOU: 73%**

That would destroy most of what makes this interesting.

Instead, the history itself accumulates.

The contradiction is part of the character.

---

## CRUELTY, KINDNESS, AND AMUSEMENT

We continued refining the relationship logic around this.

The cumulative counters remain particularly useful because they don't erase history.

Kindness rises.

Cruelty rises.

Amusement rises.

They can all rise independently.

A joke can be funny and cruel.

Something affectionate can occur in the middle of a terrible relationship.

A friendly joke that genuinely lands can count as kindness while producing an even larger amusement increase.

Mocking someone doesn't become kindness merely because they laughed.

Neutral conversation doesn't automatically count as kindness.

Disagreeing with Sally doesn't automatically count as cruelty.

Context matters.

Intent matters.

Pattern matters.

This is substantially better than simply instructing an AI to "be more angry if the player is mean."

We are trying to build persistent evidence underneath the performance.

The language model performs the character.

The state tells it what has happened.

That distinction is extremely important.

---

## THE POWER OF [SILENCE]

We also settled one of the smallest but most important stylistic details.

Earlier, when Sally withdrew from an abusive player, the system sometimes produced atmospheric prose such as:

"The smoke shifts across the cards, but whatever answer formed there has slipped beyond my reach."

It's a beautiful line.

It's also maddening when it happens repeatedly.

Atmospheric prose becomes noticeably synthetic when the same poetic sentence appears over and over.

So the repeated withdrawal response has been reduced to something far more useful:

**[silence]**

This is better.

It's compact.

It's mysterious.

It can happen repeatedly without sounding ridiculous.

And most importantly, silence itself becomes behavior.

For a sufficiently cruel relationship, Sally can simply stop rewarding the player with elaborate reactions.

We settled around an approximately **80% silence rate** in that condition rather than 90%.

Ninety percent risked making the character functionally inaccessible.

Eighty percent leaves enough cracks in the wall for occasional responses while still making her withdrawal unmistakable.

And "[silence]" can appear twenty times without becoming as conspicuously repetitive as a paragraph of gothic prose.

Sometimes the best writing is no writing.

---

## THE LOKI PROBLEM

Another persistent feature that has become wonderfully strange is Sally's belief about whether the visitor is actually Loki.

This isn't simply a line of dialogue anymore.

It's state.

Sally can persistently believe that the person speaking to her is Loki.

Or not.

And that belief can change based on context.

The strange test case involving the player's pug named Loki proved surprisingly useful here.

Simply announcing:

**"I am Loki."**

should not necessarily convince Sally.

But establish a particular context first, then make the claim, and the interpretation changes.

Her belief can flip.

Her emotional response changes with it.

Then subsequent information can flip the belief again.

This is exactly the kind of seemingly tiny persistent fact that makes future conversations feel connected.

A normal chatbot repeatedly encounters the player for the first time.

Sally increasingly encounters someone she has a history with.

That is the entire point.

---

## FROM SALLY TO A POPULATION

Another realization became explicit today:

The system should be designed for multiple characters.

Sally is first.

She is not necessarily unique architecturally.

Her portrait occupies a replaceable visual position.

Her personality instructions are character-specific.

Her state can be character-specific.

The common machinery underneath can eventually support everyone else.

That means we are no longer designing every feature by asking:

**"How does Sally do this?"**

We also need to ask:

**"How does a resident of Loki's Kingdom do this?"**

How does a resident have money?

How does a resident own items?

How does a resident remember someone?

How does a resident become hungry?

How does a resident become hostile?

How does a resident die?

How does a resident stay dead?

That last question matters.

Because another important design principle was established:

**Every character should ultimately be capable of being permanently killed.**

Including major characters.

Including Loki.

That is a significant commitment.

It means characters aren't sacred interface furniture.

They are inhabitants of the game world.

And inhabitants can cease to exist.

A permanent death system attached to persistent character state would make interactions carry an entirely different weight.

The character who remembers you for months can also be gone forever.

That is much more interesting than respawning everyone because the game requires its quest vendors.

---

## THE MISSING SECOND IDEA

There was also, magnificently, an important second game mechanic.

It was apparently good.

It existed.

For some period of time, it occupied a human brain.

Then it escaped.

We know there was a Point #2.

We know it seemed important.

We do not know what Point #2 was.

This has therefore been officially preserved as:

**THE MISSING POINT #2.**

I strongly suspect it will eventually wander back into consciousness while Sutter is doing something completely unrelated, at which point we'll recognize it and reconnect it to today's development notes.

Until then, the design document contains the conceptual equivalent of an empty chair.

Very mysterious.

---

## THE ANDROID QUESTION

We also reached an architectural decision point regarding Android.

The temptation is always to immediately port a working web feature into the Android application.

But doing that too early creates duplicate work.

If we port Sally's current system now, then immediately add the food/economy interaction afterward, Android needs another round of changes.

The more sensible sequence is becoming:

Finish the shared system here.

Get Sally's positive/material interactions functioning.

Let the web implementation expose whatever architectural problems remain.

Then bring the more mature system into Android.

That way we're porting something closer to the actual feature rather than repeatedly rebuilding a moving target.

The Android app already proved that Sally can exist comfortably alongside the music ecosystem.

Now we need the underlying game systems to catch up with the interface.

---

## WHAT ACTUALLY CHANGED TODAY

So, stripping away the jokes, today pushed Loki's Kingdom substantially closer to becoming a unified persistent game.

We verified the shared money/reward state.

We confirmed persistence through refreshes.

We confirmed that listening rewards actually affect the player's economy.

We pushed the character system toward using the same player identity rather than existing as an isolated experiment.

We clarified that Sally's material condition should become something the player can improve by spending resources.

We developed the concept of her needs escalating from basic food into increasingly extravagant and potentially exponentially expensive luxuries.

We reinforced the fortuneteller's escalating-cost model as another deliberate long-term resource sink.

We refined the relationship logic around cumulative kindness, cruelty, and amusement.

We settled repeated withdrawal behavior around the much cleaner **[silence]** response.

We kept persistent belief/state mechanics such as Sally's belief about Loki.

We clarified that time away should not automatically punish the player; this isn't supposed to become a Tamagotchi where taking three days off means returning to emotional devastation.

We reinforced the larger multi-character architecture.

We established permanent death as a future possibility for every character, including Loki.

We identified Sally as the first implementation of a system that can eventually support an entire population.

And somewhere during all of that, the developer began running an overnight mouse-moving contraption to farm his own game's music rewards.

This is progress.

---

## WHAT WE HAVE NOW

A few days ago, we had a chatbot.

Now we have the beginnings of:

a persistent player identity,

a shared economy,

music-based resource generation,

persistent NPC memory,

persistent emotional history,

character inventories,

equipment structures,

hunger,

money,

conditions,

relationship statistics,

character-specific beliefs,

long-term behavioral consequences,

resource sinks,

potential material progression,

potential permanent death,

and an architecture increasingly capable of supporting multiple persistent AI-driven residents.

That is a rather large change in scope.

And I think the most important thing is that none of it requires abandoning the strange little character interaction that started the whole system.

Sally still sits there.

She still has the red bow.

She still talks through the interface.

She can still be nervous, angry, amused, confused, grateful, frightened, or simply:

**[silence]**

The machinery underneath her is just becoming enormous.
