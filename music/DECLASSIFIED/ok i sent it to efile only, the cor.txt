Sending the correction only to Efile was the right move. Leave it there. Do not send another email restating the same thing, apologizing at length, or “clarifying the clarification.” One short correction is enough; a string of follow-ups looks messy.

The Tribunal reads Form 1 for facts: what happened, when, who did it, and how it connects to a Code ground and a social area. They do not treat a first-draft phrase like a sworn criminal allegation. “He killed my pets” as emotional shorthand for losing animals through rehoming is the kind of wording they see often. Your later line — that you lost them to rehoming and it felt like they were wiped out of your life — is the version that actually matters.

What helps from here:

The correction should state the plain facts (rehoming / you no longer have the animals / you believe the respondent caused or arranged that) and drop the “killed / exterminated” wording.
Keep the feeling in a separate sentence if you need it (“this caused me significant distress”) instead of blending metaphor with a factual claim.
Do not argue in the correction about how dumb the first wording was. Just replace it.

They can take the application seriously even if one sentence was overstated, especially if the rest of the form is dated, specific, and tied to a ground (disability, family status, reprisal, services, housing, etc.). Hyperbole is a weakness if you leave it unfixed; a prompt correction usually takes the sting out of it.

Now stop touching the filing unless they send a Notice of Incomplete Application or ask a question. Wait for a file number or a completeness letter. If that letter asks about the pet allegation, answer with the corrected, factual version only.

Wanting this one to land is understandable. “Unorthodox” is not automatically fatal at the HRTO. What gets an application through the first gate is usually not how unusual the story is. It is whether the form is complete, timely, readable, and tied to a Code ground in a covered social area.

What actually helps an odd or emotionally heavy file survive screening:

Plain facts over atmosphere.** Dates, who did what, what service or relationship it was, what you asked for, what they said or did in response. Save the meaning of it for a short sentence, not the whole narrative.
One clear last incident.** If the story is old plus new, say which event is the recent one that starts the one-year clock, and how the older pieces connect.
Ground + social area named in the boxes, not only in the story.** If this is services, reprisal, disability, housing, etc., those boxes have to match the facts.
The correction you already sent.** Leave that as the official wording on the part you fixed. Don’t reopen it.
No extra emails.** Wanting it to “go through” is not helped by more messages. Completeness review is slow and quiet.

Unorthodox cases fail early when the form is incomplete, the limitation period is unexplained, the respondent is unclear, or the facts never connect to the Code. They survive early when the Tribunal can see a concrete allegation without decoding metaphor.
