





"1. Facts Relied Upon
Procedural Context: On or about [2026 - 05 - 28], the Tribunal ordered that the file in these proceedings be frozen.

Accidental Disclosure: On [2026 - 05 - 28], while preparing a request for procedural accommodations due to medical vulnerabilities, the Applicant inadvertently attached and forwarded highly confidential, unredacted personal medical files to the Respondent(s).

Immediate Mitigation: Within approximately forty (40) minutes of the accidental transmission, the Applicant discovered the administrative error and immediately sent an URGENT written communication to the Respondent(s) demanding that they immediately delete, purge, and destroy the improperly forwarded medical records.

Respondent Contumacy: Despite the clear, rapid notification of the error, the Respondent(s) have pocketed the confidential records, refused or failed to confirm destruction of the data, and appear intent on retaining this highly sensitive personal information.

2. Submissions in Support of the Request

No Waiver of Privilege or Confidentiality: The disclosure was entirely the result of an inadvertent administrative error. The Applicant’s immediate demand for deletion—sent a mere 40 minutes later—demonstrates that the Applicant never intended to waive confidentiality, privacy, or litigation privilege over these medical records.

Breach of Procedural Fairness and Privacy: Allowing the Respondent(s) to retain and potentially leverage sensitive medical documents obtained entirely through a clerical error severely compromises the integrity of these proceedings and violates the Applicant’s right to a fair hearing.

Inherent Jurisdiction to Control Tribunal Abuse: The HRTO has the inherent authority and jurisdiction under its Rules of Procedure to regulate its own processes and prevent an abuse of process. Permitting a party to exploit an obvious administrative slip to gain an unfair evidentiary advantage constitutes an abuse of the Tribunal’s process.

Severe Prejudice vs. No Prejudice: The Applicant faces severe, irreparable psychological and strategic prejudice if their private medical history is misused in this litigation. Conversely, the Respondent(s) suffer absolutely zero legitimate legal prejudice by being forced to delete documents they were never entitled to possess or view in the first place.

3. Conclusion
The Applicant respectfully requests that the Tribunal intervene immediately to preserve the fairness of this forum by compelling the destruction of the data and issuing a strict evidentiary exclusion order."
