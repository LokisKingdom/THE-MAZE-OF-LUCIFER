Grok on Duty of Care, or the Lack Thereof:

Yes, it is still possible to bring the charge, but the "duty of care" issue is a significant hurdle you need to understand clearly.Criminal Negligence Causing Bodily Harm (s. 221)This offence requires proof of criminal negligence, which is defined in s. 219 of the Criminal Code as: Doing anything, or omitting to do anything that it is your duty to do, that shows wanton or reckless disregard for the lives or safety of other persons.
The word "duty" here means a duty imposed by law (not just a moral obligation or family expectation).Your Specific Situation
Sam is your mother’s POA (Power of Attorney for Property), not yours.
He only has legal authority/financial power over your mother’s affairs.
This means there is likely no direct legal duty imposed on him toward you personally under the POA.

This weakens the criminal negligence case because the Crown/JP must find that Sam had a legal duty toward you that he breached in a reckless way. 
When It Could Still Work
It is still possible if you can show a duty arose from other sources, such as: Sam was exercising practical control over your living situation, food, housing, or basic necessities (even if not through a formal POA for you).
He created or contributed to a situation of dependency where a duty arose (e.g., he was the one imposing the $150 food budget and controlling your access to resources).
Any other legal relationship (e.g., if he was acting in a quasi-guardian or decision-making role over you at the time).

Courts sometimes recognize duties in family/dependency situations involving extreme neglect of basic needs, but it is not automatic when there is no formal legal authority over the victim.
Realistic Advice
Criminal negligence is harder without a clear legal duty.
Aggravated assault (s. 268) might be cleaner in some ways because it does not require proving the same “duty” element — it focuses on intentionally or recklessly endangering life or causing specific harm.
Financial control/abuse angles are more commonly pursued through civil claims against the POA or specific Criminal Code sections like theft by person holding power of attorney (but that applies to the person the POA is for).

Bottom line: You can still file the private prosecution application including criminal negligence, but you should be prepared for the JP or Crown to scrutinize (and possibly reject) the duty element.
