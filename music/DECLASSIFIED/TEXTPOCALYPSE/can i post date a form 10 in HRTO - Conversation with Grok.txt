Thoughts

No. In Canada the police are not required to arrest you just because you admit something in a letter or email.Arrest is a power, not an automatic duty.
Section 495(1) of the Criminal Code says a peace officer may arrest without a warrant if they have reasonable grounds to believe an indictable offence was committed. “May” means discretion. 

laws-lois.justice.gc.ca

For many less serious offences (summary, hybrid, and some lower indictable offences), section 495(2) goes further: the officer shall not arrest without a warrant if they reasonably believe the public interest can be met without an arrest (identity known, evidence already secured, no ongoing risk) and there is no reason to think you would fail to attend court. That is an express limit on unnecessary arrests. 

canlii.org

Even when they could arrest, they can instead:take no further action
document the file and close it
refer it as a mental-health matter
recommend charges later without an immediate arrest (summons / appearance notice)
leave the charging decision to the Crown

Toronto Police procedure on persons in crisis says that if a criminal offence appears to have been committed, officers assess the circumstances and consider charges; if they do not charge, they still complete a report. That is discretion, not a mandatory arrest. 

tps.ca

A prior “mental health incident” decision does not lock the file forever.
If new information appears, police can reopen an investigation. They still do not have to arrest. Age of the event, prior finding that it was a crisis, current risk, and whether a charge is even legally available all go into that decision.Time limits matter.  Pure summary offences generally must be charged within 12 months.  
Indictable offences have no limitation period.  
Most offences are hybrid; after 12 months the Crown can often still proceed by indictment.
So “it was long ago” blocks some charges and not others. It does not create a duty to arrest.
