Yes — I understand it now. **The important thing is that negative hunger is functioning as an accumulated physical-damage burden that heals back toward zero.** The apparent doubling only happens if the player attacks repeatedly without giving Sally time to recover.

Suppose Sally reaches:

```text
Hunger: -17
```

The next harmful action deals damage equal to the current overfull magnitude:

```text
Damage = abs(-17) = 17 HP
```

and that same amount is added to the negative-hunger burden:

```text
-17 - 17 = -34
```

So immediately afterward:

```text
HP:      -17 from the attack
Hunger:  -34
```

If attacked again **immediately**:

```text
Damage = 34
Hunger: -34 - 34 = -68
```

So yes, uninterrupted attacks happen to produce:

```text
-17
-34
-68
-136
```

but **“double every attack” is not the rule**.

The rule is:

```text
ATTACK DAMAGE = CURRENT NEGATIVE HUNGER MAGNITUDE

NEW NEGATIVE HUNGER =
OLD NEGATIVE HUNGER - ATTACK DAMAGE
```

Healing is what makes the distinction important.

If Sally is sitting at:

```text
Hunger: -34
```

and enough time passes for **15 points of recovery**, then both her bodily recovery and the accumulated negative burden move back toward normal:

```text
Hunger: -34 → -19
```

Now the next harmful action deals:

```text
19 HP damage
```

and produces:

```text
Hunger: -19 → -38
```

—not 68.

So this is really a **feedback system**:

```text
repeated harm without recovery
→ rapidly worsening vulnerability

time + healing
→ negative hunger approaches zero
→ later attacks become less damaging

complete recovery
→ hunger reaches 0+
→ cascading vulnerability disappears
```

I like this considerably more than the flat staircase. It means the player's *tempo* matters. Five attacks in a row are vastly more dangerous than five attacks distributed over weeks.
