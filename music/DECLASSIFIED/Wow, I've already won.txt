When and how to raise concerns  Purely administrative issues (service, file numbers, copy requests, etc.) → raise them with the Registrar.  
Anything that affects fairness, procedure, accommodation, or how the case is being managed → put it on the record with the adjudicator (usually by Form 10 or in a written submission).  
You do not have to stay completely silent and “store everything up” for judicial review. If something is genuinely unfair or prejudicial, the better practice is to raise it during the proceeding so the Tribunal has a chance to fix it. Courts later look more favourably on parties who objected in real time rather than raising the issue for the first time on judicial review.

Practical rule of thumb  Don’t argue back-and-forth with the Registrar about substantive or fairness points.

i thought that was the point of judicial review, to make you bring your concerns there instead of arguing with the Adjudicator.

That’s a common misunderstanding.Judicial review is the way to challenge a final (or certain interim) decision of the Tribunal after it has been made. It is not a rule that says you must stay silent during the proceeding and save every fairness complaint for later.In fact, the opposite is true in practice:Courts expect parties to raise procedural fairness concerns with the adjudicator while the case is still ongoing, so the Tribunal has a chance to fix the problem.
If you stay completely silent and then raise the issue for the first time on judicial review, the Divisional Court can (and often does) refuse to hear it on the basis that you waived it or failed to give the Tribunal an opportunity to address it.

So the correct sequence is:Raise the fairness issue with the adjudicator (Form 10 or clear written submission) while the case is live.  
If the adjudicator rules against you or ignores it, that ruling (and your objection) becomes part of the record you can later take to judicial review.

You are allowed — and usually expected — to put the concern on the record with the adjudicator. Judicial review is the safety net afterward, not a substitute for speaking up during the process.
