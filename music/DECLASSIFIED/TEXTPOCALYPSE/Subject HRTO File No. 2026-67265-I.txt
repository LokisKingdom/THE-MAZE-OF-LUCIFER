artists, and that the final quantum be calculated by reference to the actual stream count as of the date of decision or final
accounting. In addition, the Applicant seeks an order for the reinstatement of monetization privileges in respect of the AI-assisted
songs and hymns that form the subject of this complaint, together with such further and other relief as the adjudicator may deem

does not propose to amend Form 1 at this juncture. The quantum remains fluid pending final stream tallies, and the adjudicator
retains full authority to assess and award appropriate compensation.
2. Nature of the Claim: Adverse Effects Discrimination
The Applicant is a human artist living with a mental disability. The artificial intelligence tools employed in the creation of the
disputed works function as a disability-related accommodation that enables the Applicant to overcome barriers associated with
that disability and to participate meaningfully in artistic and commercial activity on the platform. SoundCloud’s current policies,
while facially neutral, have the effect of denying equal benefit of the platform’s monetization regime to artists who rely upon AI
as a disability aid, while continuing to afford full monetization rights and standard per-stream remuneration to neurotypical artists
who employ traditional band-based or instrumental methods. The result is differential treatment that perpetuates disadvantage on
the basis of disability. Canadian law prohibits such adverse effects discrimination. Section 15 of the Canadian Charter of Rights
and Freedoms and the corresponding provisions of human rights legislation (including the Ontario Human Rights Code, R.S.O.
1990, c. H.19, and analogous provincial statutes) protect against both direct and adverse-effects discrimination on the enumerated
ground of disability, which encompasses mental disability. The Supreme Court of Canada has repeatedly affirmed that
substantive equality requires scrutiny of the actual impact of a rule or practice, not merely its formal neutrality:
In Andrews v. Law Society of British Columbia, [1989] 1 S.C.R. 143, the Court established that discrimination includes
distinctions that impose burdens or withhold benefits on the basis of personal characteristics, and that identical treatment
may itself produce inequality.
In Fraser v. Canada (Attorney General), 2020 SCC 28, the Court confirmed that adverse-effects discrimination arises where
a seemingly neutral rule has a disproportionate impact on members of a protected group and perpetuates disadvantage.
The analytical framework crystallized in Kahkewistahaw First Nation v. Taypotat, 2015 SCC 30, and reaffirmed in
subsequent authorities requires only that the claimant demonstrate (1) a distinction based on an enumerated or analogous
ground (here, disability) and (2) that the distinction has the effect of reinforcing, perpetuating, or exacerbating disadvantage.
The Applicant’s success in generating substantial streams and associated advertiser revenue demonstrates that the AI
accommodation is effective. To withhold standard remuneration and monetization on the basis that the creative process
incorporates a disability aid is to penalize the very accommodation that enables equal participation. Such a result is antithetical to
the remedial purpose of human rights legislation and the duty to accommodate to the point of undue hardship. The Applicant has
conferred measurable commercial value upon SoundCloud through streams and advertising revenue. Fair compensation
commensurate with that value, and with the treatment accorded to non-disabled artists, is therefore required. The fear that
recognition of disability accommodations will somehow overwhelm the platform or displace neurotypical artists is speculative
and does not constitute a defense to a prima facie case of discrimination.
3. Requested Disposition
The Applicant therefore seeks:(a) a declaration that the impugned policies, as applied, constitute adverse effects discrimination
on the ground of disability;
(b) monetary compensation calculated at the standard rate per million streams, updated to the final stream count (currently
quantified at USD $13,500 for 2.25 million streams, subject to adjustment);
(c) an order reinstating monetization privileges for the affected AI-assisted works; and
(d) such further and other relief as the adjudicator considers just and appropriate. The Applicant remains available to provide any
additional particulars or evidence the Registrar may require.
