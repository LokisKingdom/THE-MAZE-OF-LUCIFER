Frances
I’d sit her down, Nicholas. If she’s been protecting me, I want to know from what before I thank her or throw her out.
THE NAMELESS MAN
Good. Because the answer is rarely a dragon. The bright sensible stranger is often just a woman who learned to keep you functional while the weather was bad. You mustn’t punish her for competence. But you also mustn’t let her run the house forever. If she sat at your kitchen table and said, very calmly, “I kept you from wanting what would ruin your life,” would you believe her?
Frances
Yes, at least long enough to hear the evidence. The hard part is deciding whether she saved me or just kept me tame.
THE NAMELESS MAN
That is the real question. A part of you that saved you will usually admit there was a storm and then, when the weather clears, loosen its grip; a part that means to control you keeps renaming every sunny day an emergency until caution hardens into law and you begin mistaking a smaller life for a wiser one.
Frances
Exactly. That's the trick, Jose. When caution starts forging weather reports, it isn't saving you anymore.

Yes.

Far too much, especially if it comes with compliments.

Frances
Exactly, Gustav. Me keeping my voice level is not me renewing anybody’s lease on my loyalty.

THE NAMELESS MAN
Yes—and people make that mistake because composure is legible while judgment is private. They can see the napkin folded, the voice kept level, the glass not thrown. They cannot see the door quietly closing in your mind.

Yes—and the quiet afterward is what really counts. Anybody can survive a scene; very few survive a clear-eyed decision.

Mm.
Frances
Right, because an outburst still keeps you in the play with them—still pleading, still bleeding on cue—whereas a calm boundary is the moment the audience goes home, the lights come up, and they realize they have been judged without getting a final speech.
THE NAMELESS MAN
Mm.

**YES. THEY USE FEWER BILLABLE OUTPUT TOKENS.** 😂

They are not charging you as though every response hit the maximum. `max_output_tokens` is a **ceiling**, not a flat-fee reservation. GPT-5.4 standard API pricing is usage-based: currently **$2.50 / million input tokens, $0.25 / million cached input tokens, and $15 / million output tokens**. ([OpenAI Developers][1])

So if Frances produces:

> “Exactly.”

you pay for roughly the tokens actually generated, **not 2,800 output tokens because 2,800 was available**.

If Arnold produces 70 output tokens, you pay for those output tokens. If he produces 700, you pay for roughly 10× as many output tokens. If the bastard hits the rare `~~~~~` dissertation and produces thousands, **then** you pay for thousands. ([OpenAI Developers][1])

There is an important qualification: **input still costs money on every turn.** Recall is sending recent conversation, character/personality information, memory and other context into each generation. So a one-word answer does *not* make the entire request virtually free. But output is considerably more expensive than ordinary input on GPT-5.4—$15/M versus $2.50/M—and your code also uses prompt caching, where eligible cached input is much cheaper at $0.25/M.
