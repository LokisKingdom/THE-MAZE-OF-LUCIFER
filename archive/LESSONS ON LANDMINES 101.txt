> **“Deny the request in a manner that itself evidences discrimination.”**

Because an adjudicator can rule against you nine times without discriminating against you nine times. I see the architecture much more clearly now. But there's one legal distinction I'd keep very sharp because it determines whether your proposed Divisional Court sequence is powerful or weak.

The August 17 order doesn't quite say the written-submission opportunity is a personal **accommodation granted specifically to you**. It says generally that written submissions aren't required or expected, but a party *may* file them before September 14; the summary hearing then remains an opportunity for oral submissions and questions.  So I wouldn't build a future review argument around “the Tribunal granted me an accommodation and then revoked it” unless there's separate correspondence expressly granting you that accommodation.

But your underlying procedural point is stronger than that characterization.

The Tribunal has told you what the summary hearing is about. It specifically identifies nexus and the evidence reasonably available at a future merits hearing as potential questions.  It permitted written submissions addressing the legal principles, record, and summary-hearing issues.  And your filed submissions are not some unrelated 560-page manifesto dropped on an unsuspecting proceeding: the record actually contains the August 19 written submissions organized around CAMH, TPS, legal significance, evidence, and authorities. 

So if the hypothetical sequence someday became:

**written submissions accepted → materially relevant submissions disregarded for an improper reason → meaningful opportunity to address the summary-hearing issues denied → application dismissed**

then yes, *that sequence* is something I would examine extremely carefully before judicial review. Not because losing proves bias, and not automatically because the material was lengthy, but because procedural fairness questions can arise from **what opportunity you were actually afforded to present your case and whether the decision-maker genuinely dealt with the case before them**.

There's another important wrinkle: “he didn't read my 560 pages” and “he failed to consider my case” aren't necessarily equivalent. An adjudicator can understand and decide a huge record without discussing every document or argument. If you ever reached judicial review, the decision and actual procedural record would matter much more than trying to prove how many pages passed before his eyes.

And that's exactly why your current **full-record request** was sensible. You asked for the complete Tribunal record after conclusion without attaching any accusation to the request. 

On the SoundCloud/thin-skull theory, I understand what your **“To: SoundCloud”** was doing now: you weren't merely giving them an economics lecture. You were putting them on notice of your proposed causal theory connecting their alleged initial wrongdoing to downstream consequences involving CAMH and the broader events.
