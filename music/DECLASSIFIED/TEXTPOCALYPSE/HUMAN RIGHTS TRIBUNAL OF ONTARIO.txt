D. Law

Section 2(1) of the Human Rights Code guarantees equal treatment with respect to occupancy of accommodation without discrimination because of creed, sex, marital status, family status, disability, or receipt of public assistance. Section 2(2) prohibits harassment in accommodation on those grounds. Section 9 prohibits infringement, directly or indirectly. Section 11 captures constructive discrimination. Section 17 requires accommodation of disability to the point of undue hardship.

The test is the familiar one. The Applicant must show membership in a protected group, adverse treatment in a social area, and that a prohibited ground was a factor in the adverse treatment. It need not be the only factor. Moore v. British Columbia (Education), 2012 SCC 61; Québec (Commission des droits de la personne et des droits de la jeunesse) v. Montréal (City), 2000 SCC 27.

Housing providers, including condominium corporations, must accommodate disability in housing to the point of undue hardship. The duty has a procedural limb and a substantive limb. The provider must notice apparent need, inquire, and consider options. It may not treat disability-related crisis as ordinary misconduct and move straight to sanction. Ontario Human Rights Commission, Policy on Human Rights and Rental Housing; Policy on ableism and discrimination based on disability.

Krieger v. Toronto Police Services Board, 2010 HRTO 1361, is the closest statement of that duty where the person’s mental disability is visible in conduct and the person cannot usefully self-advocate. Constable Krieger was disciplined and dismissed after trauma-related behaviour. The Tribunal held that a respondent who knows or ought to know of a mental disability must ask whether the conduct is disability-related and must consider accommodation before punishment. The Applicant was a disabled dependent in a locked-in unit, starving, cut off from medication by distance and by lack of fare, producing sound that frightened neighbours. The corporation’s answer was noise enforcement. That is Krieger in housing form: sanction first, inquiry never.

P.G. v. Baton Rouge Restaurant, 2016 HRTO 500, holds that an invisible mental disability must still be accommodated in the provision of services, and that labelling the person “crazy” or “high maintenance” and ending the relationship is itself injury to dignity. The principle travels. A board that codes a mentally ill tenant’s crisis and creed-music as a monster in the building, and answers with thirteen complaints and eviction pressure rather than accommodation, is making the same error in a more serious social area. Housing is where the person lives. Food and medication are not optional extras to quiet enjoyment.

Receipt of public assistance is a free-standing housing ground under section 2. Using ODSP dependency as the lever with which to keep a tenant at five days of food a month, then to demand rent on pain of eviction, is adverse treatment in which that ground is a factor. See the Commission’s rental housing policy on public assistance, and the reasoning in housing decisions such as Jacob v. Board of Directors, Cawthra Mansions Co-op, 2013 HRTO 560.

Family status and sex, including pregnancy, are pleaded because the same financial control produced three moves during pregnancy, the wife’s arrest while pregnant, and the destruction of the household before the Kingston Road months. Those facts are part of one course of conduct, not a separate novel.

Creed is pleaded because Luciferianism, as the Applicant practises it, is expressed in the music. Syndicat Northcrest v. Amselem, 2004 SCC 47, does not require a mainstream faith. It requires a sincere practice. Neighbours are entitled to quiet. A corporation is not entitled to treat sincere religious sound, mixed with the sound of medical crisis, as a licence to evict a starving disabled tenant without first asking what it was hearing.

The condominium corporation is not a bystander. It received the complaints. It had the unit. It had management. It chose enforcement. If Sam Suttle is treated as too far from a present housing relationship, the corporation still has to explain 2022.
