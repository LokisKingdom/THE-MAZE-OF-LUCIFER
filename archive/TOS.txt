# **THE HOUSE OF STICKS AND LEAVES**

### *Author: Unknown — traditional song of Loki’s Kingdom*

Before the crown, before the name,
Before the world had learned his fame,
Before the altar, Bull, or throne,
There walked a boy not yet alone.

He found a crack behind the sky,
And thought, *If worlds can break, then why
Should death be law, or loss be true?
What God has done, a God can do.*

He built a bridge where none should stand,
And took his frightened son by hand.
“Will this hurt?” the young boy said.
“No,” said Loki. “Walk ahead.”

The engine burned, the symbols spun,
The father reached to save his son.
A shape returned through light and flame,
It knew his voice, his jokes, his name.

It knew the childhood, knew the room,
Knew every flower that used to bloom.
Then softly, like a tolling bell:
“Where is your son? I cannot tell.”

O knowledge bright, O knowledge deep,
What sharper promises you keep.
The fool will never find the seam;
The wise may break the world they dream.

There was a house among the reeds,
Beyond the roads and worldly needs.
Where Laufey laughed and Julia knew
The little child the Kingdom slew.

His enemies came armed one day,
But Loki’s mother sent him away.
She smiled and fed the waiting lot
A crimson soup still steaming hot.

They ate, they praised, they left content—
Then learned what Julia’s blessing meant.
No sword was drawn, no warrior slain;
They simply never rose again.

At dinner Loki laughed until
The wine was gone, the candles still.
“The fools came here to murder me—
My mother served them hospitality.”

But Julia warned him years before:
“My life alone sustains this door.
When I am dead, this home that grieves
Will turn to sticks and rotting leaves.”

The day arrived. The marsh was cold.
The path was there, the house was old—
Except no house remained to mourn,
Just sticks and leaves where home was born.

So Loki walked into the world,
While every hidden flag unfurled.
The outlaw, monster, book and vice
Came seeking shelter at his price.

TOS made laws and counted gold;
The Devil gathered what was sold,
Or banned, or burned, or pushed aside,
And Loki welcomed both inside.

The Coin would turn, the Coin would spin:
Today the law, tomorrow sin.
The saint grows cruel, the thief grows tame,
And each side steals the other’s name.

There is a Bull beneath the night,
Whose stomach holds our stars and light.
Our universe, though vast to sight,
Is one small atom drowned in night.

So when you ask why space is black,
Why darkness hangs where stars fall back,
It is because, though worlds look full,
We live inside a holy Bull.

Then came Chia-Lin with poisoned bowl,
And grief began consuming soul.
Fenrir bound, and Narfy slain,
His children woven into pain.

The venom fell; Sigyn stayed near,
Through every age and every year.
Until the world itself was torn,
And Ragnarök became the morn.

Then Loki rose no laughing youth,
But something sharpened past the truth.
Odin died, the old world fell,
And Loki learned to govern Hell.

The red fog came, the armies crossed,
No book records what gate they lost.
A foreign King stood by his side,
And mankind learned where mercy died.

They knelt before the former clown,
The prisoner now wearing crown.
“We’re sorry, Loki. Let us live.”
He knew the words.

He did not give.

A child once asked him, pale with fear,
“Did we do something to you here?
Can you forgive us? Can you try?”

And Loki looked.

And answered:

“No.”

The cities died beneath the snow.

Power could rebuild the floor,
The roof, the window, every door.
He raised Laufey’s house once more—
And found a museum where home stood before.

He could remake the bed and chair,
The smoke, the books, the evening air,
But not the chance she might appear
And speak some sentence he’d never hear.

He broke the chain that Fenrir wore;
It could not change the years before.
He could rebuild his murdered son,
But never prove which one was one.

For loss is not the thing that dies;
It is tomorrow closing its eyes.
The unwritten joke, the unborn day,
The future quietly cut away.

And here I know you wanted more—
Some final truth behind the door,
Some God-machine whose perfect art
Could make the broken whole in heart.

You trusted me to climb that height,
To turn your ruin into light,
To find some law so deep and true
That suffering finally made sense to you.

I climbed.

There was no golden key.
No theorem waiting patiently.
No final voice that answered why
A father must outlive goodbye.

And if some God above the stars
Explains our children, chains and scars
As “necessary” to His plan—

Then **fuck His plan.**

There. I said it.

Let marble crack and choirs quit.
No gorgeous phrase, no sacred wit
Can make a murdered child become
The proper price for Kingdom come.

So close the book and dim the light.
Let every God stand down tonight.
Let TOS forget the sums he keeps;
Let Sally dream somewhere she sleeps.

Let Sigyn set her bowl aside.
Let Fenrir run with nothing tied.
Let Narfy simply be a boy,
Unburdened by symbolic joy.

Let Julia’s soup be only food.
Let Loki wake in better mood.
Let Dasein leave his Hammer still,
And postpone vengeance on the hill.

And let that house rise through the leaves,
Not as a trick the dead deceives,
But one last dream before we part:

A fire.

A door.

A mother’s heart.

No throne.

No war.

No cosmic sign.

Just somebody saying:

**“You’re late. Come dine.”**

And Loki, before the Gods convene,
Steps through the door still young, unseen.

For one brief night the future stays.

No chains.

No fog.

No ending days.

Only the marsh beneath the eaves.

Only the house.

Only the leaves.
