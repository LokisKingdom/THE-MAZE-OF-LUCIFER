Commercial / restaurant services (closest parallel)P.G. v. Baton Rouge Restaurant (Groupe Restaurant Imvescor Restaurant Group Inc.), 2016 HRTO 500  Applicant had PTSD, obsessive-compulsive disorder, and germaphobia (mental disabilities).  
He had previously been accommodated with specific seating, cleaning, and service requests related to his conditions.  
New management refused the accommodations, called him “high maintenance,” ordered him to leave, and the manager said words to the effect of: “Now I know why the police shoot crazy people like you.”  
HRTO found discrimination in services on the ground of disability, rejected any justification based on policy or convenience, and awarded $12,000 for injury to dignity, feelings and self-respect.

This is the strongest publicly reported example of a person winning after being denied (or effectively ejected from) a commercial service because of behaviour/presentation tied to mental illness.Police / mental-disability presentationKrieger v. Toronto Police Services Board, 2010 HRTO 1361  New constable developed PTSD after a traumatic on-duty incident.  
His behaviour changed (over-reaction, unusual conduct). Supervisors suspected a mental-health issue but did not inquire properly or accommodate; they suspended him as “unfit” and ultimately terminated him.  
HRTO found the Toronto Police discriminated by failing both the procedural and substantive duty to accommodate a mental disability, even though the officer himself did not fully recognize or request accommodation at the time. Reinstatement and policy remedies ordered.
