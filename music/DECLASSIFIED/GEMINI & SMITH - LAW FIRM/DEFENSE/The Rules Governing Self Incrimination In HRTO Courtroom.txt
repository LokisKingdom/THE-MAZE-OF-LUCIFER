At the Human Rights Tribunal of Ontario (HRTO), there is generally no "right to remain silent" in the American constitutional sense, as Ontario law follows a "use immunity" model. If the opposing party asks a question that might incriminate you or establish liability, the correct legal response usually involves a strategic combination of objecting on the record and asserting your statutory use immunity under the Ontario Evidence Act.

When you are asked to provide self-incriminating testimony at the HRTO:
1. The Legal Reality (Use Immunity)
In Ontario, a witness cannot refuse to answer a question merely because the answer might incriminate them. However, under Subsection 9(1) of the Ontario Evidence Act, you must answer the question, but the law strictly prohibits the use of your compelled answers against you in any other provincial or civil proceedings.

2. What to Say in the Moment
If you are unrepresented and faced with an inappropriate or unjustified question, the correct immediate response is to address the adjudicator:
Object to relevance: State, "I object to this question. There is insufficient evidence to justify this line of questioning, and it is irrelevant to the issues properly before the Tribunal."
