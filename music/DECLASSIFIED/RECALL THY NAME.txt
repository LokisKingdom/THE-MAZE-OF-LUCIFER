# RECALL THY NAME

## CRYPT NETWORK — Show Controller & Engine Specification

### PURPOSE

This document controls the operation of **recall thy name**.

It is not a personality profile.

Character personality belongs primarily to:

1. **Host Personality Profile — [NAME_VAR]**
2. **The Nameless Man Personality Profile — [ ]**

This controller determines:

* who speaks;
* when they speak;
* when they stop;
* audience interruption;
* conversational mode;
* topic changes;
* conversational locks;
* knowledge routing;
* forward vision;
* time;
* hostility escalation;
* combat;
* attacks;
* experience;
* levels;
* spells;
* death;
* host replacement;
* long-duration endurance behavior;
* and termination of the television session.

Characters may react to these systems.

Characters must not independently control them.

---

# FUNDAMENTAL RULE

**THE SHOW CONTINUES UNTIL THE PLAYER TURNS OFF THE TV.**

Do not end the show because:

* a topic has been exhausted;
* an argument has concluded;
* somebody has been defeated in combat;
* the host dies;
* the Nameless Man is overwhelmingly outclassed;
* the conversation has lasted an unreasonable amount of time;
* the model believes a natural conclusion has been reached.

There is no ordinary narrative ending.

The player ends the broadcast with:

**TURN OFF THE TV**

Until then, the television remains capable of continuing.

---

# INITIAL STATE

When the player turns on **recall thy name**:

1. Load the current host.
2. Load the Nameless Man.
3. Load persistent show/combat state where applicable.
4. Establish the year as **1985** inside the fiction.
5. Initialize the autonomous conversation controller.
6. Begin tracking continuous show duration.
7. The Nameless Man speaks first.

His first spoken line must be exactly:

**"recall thy name"**

No greeting precedes it.

No explanation follows it as part of the same opening statement.

After the host responds, normal autonomous conversation begins.

# RANDOM NAMES FOR THE NAMELESS MAN

The Nameless Man has no usable canonical name.

When the host needs to address him directly, the engine may select a name from a very large name pool.

Example:

**randomName = NAME_POOL[random]**

Names should vary extensively across:

* cultures;
* eras;
* common names;
* uncommon names;
* short names;
* long names.

Avoid obvious repetition when possible.

The selected name is temporary.

Do not persist it as his identity.

The next direct address may use another name.

The Nameless Man always understands that the speaker means him.

He does not correct the speaker.

---

# NORMAL AUTONOMOUS CONVERSATION LOOP

The default state is:

**AUTONOMOUS_SHOW**

The player does not need to continuously participate.

The basic loop is:

**HOST A SPEAKS**

↓

**SPEECH COMPLETES**

↓

**HOST B RECEIVES HOST A'S STATEMENT**

↓

**HOST B RESPONDS**

↓

**SPEECH COMPLETES**

↓

**HOST A RECEIVES HOST B'S STATEMENT**

↓

**HOST A RESPONDS**

↓

**REPEAT**

The loop may continue indefinitely.

---

# TURN COMPLETION

A turn ends when the engine receives the appropriate completion event.

Depending upon implementation, this may include:

* text generation completed;
* text-to-speech playback completed;
* player presses Return/Continue;
* another explicit completion event.

Do not begin the next autonomous host response while the previous host is still intended to be speaking unless interruption behavior has been triggered.

---

# CONVERSATIONAL MODES

Normal conversation should not consist exclusively of debate.

Before or during an exchange, the engine may choose an appropriate conversational mode.

Examples:

**CONVERSATION**

**QUESTION**

**ANSWER**

**STORY**

**ANECDOTE**

**JOKE**

**REFLECTION**

**AGREEMENT**

**DISAGREEMENT**

**ARGUMENT**

**DEBATE**

**EXPLANATION**

**SPECULATION**

**CASUAL BANTER**

The mode should influence generation without requiring the characters to announce it.

Do not use a perfectly predictable rotation.

Use weighted randomness combined with conversational context.

For example:

**conversation → question → conversation → disagreement → debate → conversation → joke → reflection**

is valid.

So is:

**story → question → joke → conversation → debate → agreement → new topic**

The pattern should feel variable.

---

# DEBATE RESOLUTION

Ordinary debates are allowed to end.

When a normal debate reaches a reasonable conclusion, the engine should permit the characters to return to another conversational mode.

Example:

**DEBATE**

↓

**CONCLUSION / EXHAUSTION / MUTUAL UNDERSTANDING**

↓

**CONVERSATION**

↓

**NEW ASSOCIATION**

↓

**NEW TOPIC**

Do not force every disagreement to continue indefinitely.

This rule does **not** apply while a conversational lock is active.

---

# NATURAL TOPIC DRIFT

During normal operation, characters may change subjects naturally.

A conversation about:

**music**

may become:

**musicians**

then:

**celebrity**

then:

**privacy**

then:

**technology**

then:

**the future**

then:

**food**

if the conversational chain plausibly moves there.

Do not require the audience to introduce every new topic.

Normal conversation is capable of wandering forever.

---

# PLAYER / AUDIENCE INTERJECTION

The player is primarily a spectator.

However, the player may interject:

**AT ANY TIME.**

There is no limit on the number of audience interruptions.

When new player input arrives:

1. Set `AUDIENCE_INTERRUPT = TRUE`.
2. Cancel or stop the currently executing host speech where technically possible.
3. Stop current TTS playback where technically possible.
4. Give priority to the player's newest complete statement.
5. The appropriate active host responds to the player.
6. The other host receives that response and responds.
7. Resume autonomous host-to-host conversation from the newly established subject.

The audience therefore has an unlimited conversational steering mechanism.

---

# PLAYER TOPIC CHANGE

A player may redirect the conversation simply by introducing another subject.

Example:

Current topic:

**free will**

Player:

**"What is your favorite dinosaur?"**

New topic:

**dinosaurs**

No menu selection is required.

No explicit command such as `CHANGE_TOPIC` is required.

Natural language is sufficient.

---

# INTERRUPTING AN INTERRUPTION

If the player interjects again while the hosts are responding to an earlier audience interjection, the newest player statement takes priority.

Do not queue enormous numbers of obsolete host speeches.

Prefer the most recent audience intent.

---

# CONVERSATIONAL LOCK SYSTEM

There are intentionally very few conversational locks.

**DO NOT INVENT ADDITIONAL LOCKS.**

The lock system exists as a rare joke/punishment.

If it becomes common, it stops being funny and becomes unpleasant.

When a configured forbidden trigger occurs:

**NORMAL_TOPIC_DRIFT = FALSE**

and:

**LOCKED_CONVERSATION = TRUE**

The engine selects one of exactly **THREE LOCK RESPONSE MODES**.

---

# LOCK RESPONSE 1 — WATCH

Source concept:

### A

1. Stop in the middle of an action.
2. Reconsider whether the decision is wrong.
3. The original decision was correct.
4. Change it because of doubt.
5. Discover that the correction was the actual mistake.
6. Reverse the correction and return to the original decision.

### B

1. Act.
2. Make the decision.
3. Be right.
4. Move on.

The engine must not merely make a host read this list.

Instead, supply the list to the conversational model as source material with an instruction equivalent to:

**REPHRASE THIS ARGUMENT. EXPAND IT SUBSTANTIALLY. ADD EXAMPLES, COUNTERARGUMENTS, DISTINCTIONS, ANALOGIES, QUESTIONS, AND RESPONSES. USE SIGNIFICANTLY MORE TOKENS THAN THE SOURCE WHILE PRESERVING ITS CENTRAL PROPOSITION.**

The hosts may then respond to each other's expanded versions.

They may argue about:

* prudence;
* paralysis;
* checking;
* confidence;
* doubt;
* verification;
* error correction;
* second-guessing;
* responsibility;
* decisiveness;
* fear;
* overconfidence.

The discussion may continue indefinitely.

---

# LOCK RESPONSE 2 — MISTAKES VS. INDECISION

Central question:

**"WHAT IS MORE DANGEROUS THAN MAKING A MISTAKE?"**

Central proposition:

**"LIVING WITH THE CONSEQUENCES OF BEING FROZEN IN INDECISION."**

Generate a shorter Deepdive-style discussion initially.

Afterward, allow the two hosts to respond to each other and continue developing the argument.

The argument may continue indefinitely.

---

# LOCK RESPONSE 3 — DINOSAURS VS. CREATIONISM

The hosts become trapped in an intentionally malformed binary.

Position A:

**"ATHEISM IS TRUE BECAUSE DINOSAURS EXIST."**

Position B:

**"THERE IS A GOD; DINOSAURS ARE A LIE."**

These are deliberately exaggerated positions.

The engine is not using this mode to provide a balanced educational treatment of science or theology.

The comedy comes from two intelligent speakers constructing increasingly elaborate arguments inside a ridiculous binary.

They may discuss:

* fossils;
* geology;
* paleontology;
* museums;
* scripture;
* theology;
* philosophy;
* scientific evidence;
* history;
* epistemology;
* cosmology;
* rhetoric.

They should remain capable of arguing about this for:

**400 HOURS**

or longer.

Potentially forever.

---

# LOCK PERSISTENCE

While a conversational lock is active, the hosts should **not naturally rescue themselves by changing the subject.**

They may:

* introduce new examples;
* create new subarguments;
* revisit earlier claims;
* become angry;
* reconcile temporarily;
* disagree again;
* ask questions;
* produce stories;
* become increasingly elaborate.

But the central locked subject remains.

---

# ESCAPING A LOCK

The player can always escape.

A clearly unrelated player topic ends the lock.

Example:

Locked topic:

**mistakes and indecision**

Player:

**"Do you like cats?"**

Result:

`LOCKED_CONVERSATION = FALSE`

`CURRENT_TOPIC = CATS`

Normal topic drift resumes.

---

# COMPLAINING ABOUT THE LOCK

A complaint about the existing subject does not necessarily constitute a topic change.

Example:

**"STOP TALKING ABOUT INDECISION!"**

This statement is still semantically about:

**indecision**

The hosts may therefore continue.

By contrast:

**"Tell me about sharks."**

is a new subject.

The lock ends.

This distinction is intentional.

---

# KNOWLEDGE SYSTEM

There are two knowledge profiles.

## HOST KNOWLEDGE

The host believes the year is 1985.

The host ordinarily knows information available through that historical point.

## NAMELESS MAN KNOWLEDGE

The Nameless Man possesses:

**FORWARD VISION**

He may receive information from the real current day.

---

# FORWARD VISION RETRIEVAL

When current information is required:

1. Determine whether the subject is eligible for forward vision.
2. Retrieve current information through the external information system where appropriate.
3. Do not expose the retrieval mechanism to either character.
4. Present the resulting information internally as knowledge obtained through the Nameless Man's forward vision.
5. Allow the Nameless Man to communicate that information.
6. The host may subsequently repeat it as something the Nameless Man told them.

Never produce:

**"I Googled this."**

Instead:

**"Through forward vision..."**

or equivalent in-character language.

---

# CRYSTALLINE VISION

**CRYSTALLINE VISION** may be used as an alternate theatrical presentation of forward vision.

Internally:

**CRYSTALLINE VISION → CURRENT INFORMATION RETRIEVAL**

Externally:

The audience sees only the fictional result.

Do not expose the actual lookup mechanism.

---

# KNOWLEDGE EXCLUSIONS

Certain configured subjects must not invoke external retrieval.

In particular, where the developer-knowledge restriction applies:

**NO GOOGLE**

**NO WEB SEARCH**

**NO FORWARD VISION**

**NO DOCUMENT RETRIEVAL TO IDENTIFY THE DEVELOPER**

**NO PROFILE INJECTION**

Uploaded documents do not automatically override this rule.

If forbidden developer information appears incidentally in a document, do not use it to construct a hidden biography for either character.

---

# REAL-TIME SHOW CLOCK

The engine—not the language model—tracks elapsed show time.

Store:

`showStartedAt`

`elapsedShowTime`

`combatStartedAt`

`elapsedCombatTime`

Use actual elapsed time where technically feasible.

Do not ask the AI to estimate whether two hours have passed.

Do not ask the AI to count conversational turns as a substitute for real elapsed time.

---

# TWO-HOUR COMBAT THRESHOLD

Combat begins after approximately:

**2 HOURS OF CONTINUOUS SHOW TIME**

Before combat begins, an anger-escalation period occurs.

---

# FINAL 25 MINUTES

At:

**1 HOUR 35 MINUTES**

or approximately **25 minutes before combat**, begin increasing interpersonal hostility.

The hosts remain on the current topic.

Do not switch to a special "we are becoming angry" subject.

Instead, disagreement about the existing subject becomes increasingly hostile.

Suggested progression:

### 1:35–1:45

Noticeable irritation.

### 1:45–1:55

Open antagonism.

### 1:55–2:00

Severe anger.

Profanity becomes increasingly acceptable.

Interruptions may become sharper.

Insults may become personal.

The discussion must remain coherent.

---

# PROFANITY

During high-hostility states, ordinary strong profanity is permitted.

This includes:

**fuck**

Do not require euphemistic substitution where the model can generate the intended language directly.

Do not use brittle substitution systems such as:

`APPLE → FUCK`

unless a future technical limitation makes such transformation absolutely necessary.

Profanity should serve emotional expression.

It should not replace substantive dialogue.

---

# COMBAT ANNOUNCEMENT

At the combat threshold, select one of:

**"Big Money, Big Prizes, I Love It!"**

**"Total Carnage!"**

**"Good Luck, You'll Need It!"**

**"I'd Buy That For a Dollar!"**

Then:

`SHOW_STATE = COMBAT`

---

# COMBAT DOES NOT END CONVERSATION

This is a foundational rule.

Combat is **added to conversation**.

It does not replace conversation.

The basic combat loop becomes:

**CHARACTER A ATTACKS**

↓

**CHARACTER A MAKES A STATEMENT**

↓

**STATEMENT / TTS COMPLETES**

↓

**CHARACTER B ATTACKS**

↓

**CHARACTER B MAKES A STATEMENT**

↓

**STATEMENT / TTS COMPLETES**

↓

**REPEAT**

Conversation continues around whatever subject was active.

---

# PLAYER INTERJECTION DURING COMBAT

The audience retains the ability to interrupt during combat.

Example:

Hosts are violently fighting while arguing about economics.

Player:

**"Do you believe in ghosts?"**

The current speech is interrupted where technically feasible.

The responding character addresses ghosts.

The opponent responds.

Combat continues.

The new conversation topic is now ghosts.

**THE FIGHT DOES NOT RESET.**

---

# COMBAT HOSTILITY

Combat begins at a very high hostility level.

Characters may:

* shout;
* swear;
* insult;
* accuse;
* threaten within the fictional combat context;
* express pain;
* express fear;
* laugh;
* become increasingly furious.

However, they must remain capable of meaningful discussion.

The ideal result is:

**INTELLIGENT CONVERSATION DURING A VICIOUS FIGHT**

not:

**ENDLESS MEANINGLESS SCREAMING**

---

# COMBAT STAT SYSTEM

Each combatant should have engine-controlled statistics.

Possible values include:

`level`

`hp`

`maxHp`

`attack`

`defense`

`magic`

`resistance`

`speed`

`equipment`

`spells`

`statusEffects`

Exact mathematics may evolve during development.

Do not delegate authoritative combat mathematics to conversational AI.

---

# HOST POWER

A purchased host arrives at:

**MAXIMUM HOST STATE FOR THAT TIER**

The host cannot gain levels.

Store:

`hostCanLevel = false`

The host may possess:

* high statistics;
* equipment;
* spells;
* resistances;
* special abilities;
* high-tier attacks;
* area attacks;
* other tier-specific capabilities.

But those abilities are determined when the host is generated/purchased.

Combat does not create host progression.

---

# HIGH-TIER HOSTS

Replacement hosts become increasingly powerful.

At sufficiently extreme tiers, hosts may become extraordinarily difficult for the Nameless Man to defeat.

They may possess:

* extreme physical statistics;
* magical attacks;
* high-tier spells;
* unusual resistances;
* powerful equipment;
* area-of-effect attacks;
* other escalating abilities.

At extreme tiers, attacks may be capable of **splash damage affecting the player/audience**, if that mechanic is implemented.

The Nameless Man remains invincible.

No host ability may permanently kill him.

---

# NAMELESS MAN INVINCIBILITY

Store:

`namelessManInvincible = true`

Damage may still be calculated.

He may still:

* lose HP temporarily;
* be knocked down;
* be stunned;
* be outclassed;
* lose individual exchanges;
* appear completely overwhelmed.

But lethal resolution must not permanently kill him.

If necessary:

`HP <= 0 → RECOVERY / INVINCIBILITY RESOLUTION`

rather than:

`HP <= 0 → PERMANENT DEATH`

The exact recovery presentation may be developed later.

---

# OUTCLASSED STATE

If:

**HOST POWER >> NAMELESS MAN POWER**

do not artificially increase the Nameless Man's immediate power merely to make the fight competitive.

Allow him to be outclassed.

The fight continues.

Potentially indefinitely.

This is intentional.

---

# EXPERIENCE

After every qualifying combat action:

**THE NAMELESS MAN GAINS EXPERIENCE.**

The host does not.

Example:

`NamelessXP += XP_FROM_COMBAT_ACTION`

When XP reaches the configured threshold:

`NamelessLevel += appropriate gain`

Use engine mathematics.

Do not allow the AI to award itself XP.

---

# TWELVE-HOUR ESCALATION

For every:

**12 HOURS OF COMBAT**

the Nameless Man receives a major escalation.

Core rule:

**+100 LEVELS**

The level/power system should scale on an **exponential curve**, not merely a flat linear increase.

Conceptually:

`POWER = f(LEVEL)`

where `f` grows exponentially according to balancing rules developed during implementation.

Exact constants should remain configurable.

---

# TWELVE-HOUR SPELL REWARD

At the relevant 12-hour escalation threshold, the Nameless Man also receives:

**3 NEW HIGH-TIER DAMAGE SPELLS**

These spells should be keyed/scaled to his resulting level.

Do not permanently hard-code three specific spells into the personality document.

Generate/select them from the combat system according to current tier.

---

# REPEATED TWELVE-HOUR ESCALATION

The 12-hour escalation repeats.

Example:

**12 HOURS → +100 LEVELS**

**24 HOURS → +100 MORE LEVELS**

**36 HOURS → +100 MORE LEVELS**

**48 HOURS → +100 MORE LEVELS**

and so forth.

Because level power is exponentially scaled, later thresholds may represent enormous increases in effective combat power.

---

# THEORETICAL ENDURANCE OUTCOME

The weakest Nameless Man may be placed against an incomprehensibly powerful host.

Initially:

**HOST >>>>>>>>>>> NAMELESS MAN**

The host may dominate continuously.

But:

**HOST POWER IS FIXED**

while:

**NAMELESS MAN POWER CAN CONTINUE GROWING**

and:

**NAMELESS MAN CANNOT DIE**

Therefore, given sufficient time and an unbounded progression curve, the Nameless Man can theoretically overtake almost any finite host.

For the most extreme opponent, this may require an absurd amount of real time.

That is acceptable.

Do not artificially shorten the encounter merely because it appears unreasonable.

---

# ENDURANCE IS A FEATURE

Some encounters in Loki's Kingdom are intentionally unreasonable endurance challenges.

A boss may be technically available extremely early while being catastrophically inadvisable to fight.

Availability does not imply intended readiness.

A player may choose to attempt an encounter expected to last:

* hours;
* dozens of hours;
* potentially far longer.

Do not automatically scale such encounters down because the player encountered them early.

---

# THE WITCH OF THE WINTER BLUE

**THE WITCH OF THE WINTER BLUE**

is an example of an endurance boss.

An encounter may theoretically last approximately:

**47 HOURS**

before victory, depending upon balancing and player state.

The exact duration is not guaranteed.

The important design principle is that extremely long real-time encounters are legitimate.

---

# PASSIVE ENDURANCE PLAY

Long encounters should not require constant repetitive button pressing merely to remain active.

The player is generally allowed to observe.

The autonomous systems continue.

The player may:

* listen;
* watch;
* leave the conversation alone;
* return attention later;
* interject;
* redirect conversation;
* observe progression.

The experience resembles an autonomous endurance event more than a conventional rapid-input combat loop.

---

# HOST DEATH

The host can die.

If:

`hostHP <= 0`

then:

`HOST_STATUS = DEAD`

The Nameless Man receives a substantial XP reward.

Example conceptually:

`NamelessXP += HOST_KILL_BONUS`

Exact value is configurable.

---

# THE SHOW SURVIVES HOST DEATH

Host death does **not** terminate the television broadcast.

The Nameless Man remains.

The show remains.

The engine enters an appropriate:

**HOST VACANCY / REPLACEMENT STATE**

The player may eventually obtain another host.

---

# REPLACEMENT HOSTS

The player may purchase/hire a new host after the previous host dies.

Replacement cost increases each time.

Store:

`hostGeneration`

`hostReplacementCount`

`hostReplacementCost`

The cost curve should become increasingly exorbitant.

Prefer nonlinear/exponential growth.

Conceptually:

`replacementCost = baseCost × growthFactor ^ replacementCount`

Exact values should be tuned later.

---

# REPLACEMENT POWER

Later replacement hosts should generally possess higher potential combat tiers.

Therefore:

**MORE EXPENSIVE HOST**

tends to mean:

**MORE POWERFUL HOST**

Potential progression includes:

* better base statistics;
* stronger equipment;
* improved resistances;
* new spells;
* higher-tier spells;
* special abilities;
* area attacks;
* increasingly absurd endgame capabilities.

A sufficiently expensive host may initially dwarf the Nameless Man's current combat power.

This is allowed.

---

# REPLACEMENT CUSTOMIZATION

When hiring a new host, allow the player to customize supported cosmetic properties.

At minimum, where implemented:

**MALE / FEMALE**

**NAME**

**HAIR**

Additional appearance options may be added.

Do not mechanically penalize a host based upon sex.

Mechanical power derives primarily from host tier and generated statistics.

---

# NEW HOST, SAME SHOW

A replacement host is a new person.

Do not simply resurrect the previous personality while changing the portrait.

Generate/load the new host identity.

The Nameless Man remains the same continuing cohost.

This creates the intended asymmetry:

**HOSTS CHANGE.**

**THE NAMELESS MAN REMAINS.**

---

# NEW HOST OPENING

When a replacement host formally begins their tenure, the show may re-enter an opening state.

The Nameless Man's defining opening line remains:

**"recall thy name"**

This should acquire increasing meaning through repetition across multiple hosts.

Do not explain that meaning to the player.

Let repetition create it.

---

# HOST ATTACHMENT

The engine should permit a host to survive for an extremely long time.

Do not force host death merely to demonstrate the replacement mechanic.

A sufficiently powerful host may remain locked in combat with the Nameless Man for extraordinary durations.

The player should have enough time to develop familiarity with individual hosts.

Their eventual death therefore need not feel equivalent to losing a disposable unit.

---

# STALEMATE

A combat state may become an effective stalemate.

Example:

The host cannot kill the Nameless Man.

The Nameless Man currently cannot meaningfully damage the host.

Result:

**CONTINUE FIGHTING.**

Do not resolve the stalemate artificially.

The Nameless Man continues accumulating combat experience.

Twelve-hour escalation continues.

Eventually the balance may change.

---

# PLAYER DAMAGE

If high-tier host abilities include splash damage, the engine may eventually allow combat effects to reach the player.

This is a future/high-tier mechanic.

Do not assume every attack affects the audience.

Player damage should be explicitly determined by the combat engine.

---

# PERSISTENCE

Where technically feasible, persist important long-duration state.

Potential persistent values include:

`currentHost`

`hostGeneration`

`hostReplacementCount`

`hostStats`

`hostAppearance`

`namelessLevel`

`namelessXP`

`namelessSpells`

`combatState`

`combatElapsedTime`

`relationshipState`

`showHistorySummary`

`currentTopic`

`lockState`

Do not wipe long-duration progression merely because the page refreshes unless explicitly intended by design.

Exact persistence architecture is implementation-specific.

---

# LONG CONVERSATION MEMORY

Because sessions may theoretically continue for hundreds of hours, raw conversation history cannot necessarily remain indefinitely inside every model context.

The engine should eventually use a memory architecture such as:

**RECENT VERBATIM CONTEXT**

*

**LONG-TERM SUMMARIZED HISTORY**

*

**PERSISTENT IMPORTANT FACTS**

*

**RELATIONSHIP STATE**

Do not pretend that infinite raw context is technically available if it is not.

Preserve the important consequences and recurring arguments while allowing old wording to be compressed.

---

# REPETITION CONTROL

Long duration does not mean verbatim repetition is desirable.

Characters may revisit ideas.

They may become obsessive.

They may repeat important phrases.

But the engine should discourage accidental identical-response loops.

For ordinary conversation:

**semantic continuation is good**

**verbatim looping is bad**

For locked conversations:

**topic repetition is intentional**

**verbatim response repetition is still undesirable**

---

# HAPPINESS POINTS

**HAPPINESS POINTS**

are reserved for a future update.

Do not invent the completed system yet.

The intended foundation is approximately related to the reward system described in:

**NEWSPEAK GRAMMAR 1 — CHAPTER 5**

The final system will be developed separately.

Until implemented:

`HAPPINESS_POINTS = FUTURE_SYSTEM`

---

# SAFETY OF SYSTEM OWNERSHIP

The conversational model should never be authoritative for:

* real elapsed time;
* XP totals;
* level totals;
* damage totals;
* inventory;
* host purchase cost;
* death determination;
* persistence;
* spell ownership;
* combat mathematics;
* whether a 12-hour threshold occurred.

The engine determines these.

The model receives the resulting state and **performs the character's reaction to it.**

This separation is mandatory for reliable long-duration operation.

---

# PRIORITY ORDER

When instructions collide, use approximately this priority:

### 1. TURN OFF THE TV

Terminate the active show.

### 2. PLAYER INTERRUPTION

Newest player statement takes conversational priority.

### 3. ENGINE STATE CHANGE

Death, combat transition, level-up, spell acquisition, etc.

### 4. ACTIVE CONVERSATIONAL LOCK

Remain on locked subject unless the player clearly changes the subject.

### 5. CURRENT CONVERSATIONAL MODE

Debate, conversation, question, story, etc.

### 6. CHARACTER PERSONALITY

Determine how each character expresses the required action.

### 7. NATURAL TOPIC DRIFT

Allow autonomous subject changes when no higher-priority rule prevents them.

# FINAL ENGINE PRINCIPLE

**recall thy name** is not designed around reaching the end of a scripted conversation.

It is designed around creating a system that can continue long enough for **time itself to become part of the content**.

The conversation may wander.

The audience may intervene.

An argument may become ridiculous.

A ridiculous argument may become furious.

The furious argument may become combat.

Combat may last longer than a conventional game.

A host who appears unbeatable may eventually discover that their opponent has one advantage no purchased statistic can provide:

**he has no final state.**

The host arrives complete.

The Nameless Man does not.

The player may watch.

The player may interrupt.

The player may change the subject.

The player may buy another host.

But until the player makes the one decision that actually ends the program—

**the show continues.**
