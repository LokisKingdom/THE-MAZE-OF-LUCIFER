No, you cannot use Japanese characters (kanji, hiragana, or katakana) in your official corporate name in Ontario.Ontario Rules (Business Corporations Act + Business Names Act):The corporate name must use the Roman alphabet (A–Z, a–z) + Arabic numerals + limited punctuation.
The first character must be a letter or number.
If you try to include Japanese characters, they will reject it. You would have to register an English (or French) version.

You can use Japanese characters in marketing, logos, websites, or trade names, but the legal corporate name on the incorporation documents has to be Roman alphabet only.So for “Lucifer Corporation”, stick with the English spelling if you want it to go through smoothly.Want me to help brainstorm slight variations that keep the spirit if “Lucifer Corporation” gets flagged for other reasons? Federally (Canada-wide incorporation):Still no — you cannot use Japanese characters (kanji, hiragana, katakana) in the official corporate name.Federal Rules (Canada Business Corporations Act):Corporate names must use the Roman alphabet (A–Z).
They allow some punctuation and symbols, but the name has to be readable in English or French using standard Latin characters.
Non-Latin scripts are not permitted for the legal corporate name.

Same situation as Ontario: you could use Japanese text in branding, domain, logos, etc., but the official name registered with Corporations Canada must be in Roman letters.Bottom line:
